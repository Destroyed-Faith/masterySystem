## Passive Design Rules

Passives represent reliable, always-on character identity.  
They require no action to activate, but they must be chosen before combat according to the Passive Loadout rules.

## Passive Curve

Passives use the following PP target curve:

| **Level** | **Target PP** |
|:--:|--:|
| **1** | 20 |
| **2** | 40 |
| **3** | 60 |
| **4** | 80 |
| **5** | 100 |
| **6** | 120 |
| **7** | 140 |
| **8** | 160 |
| **9** | 180 |
| **10** | 200 |
| **11** | 220 |
| **12** | 240 |
| **13** | 260 |
| **14** | 280 |
| **15** | 300 |
| **16** | 320 |

#### Passive Slot Progression

Characters gain Passive Slots by Mastery Rank.

| **Mastery Rank** | **Passive Slots** |
|:--:|:--:|
| **MR 1** | 1 |
| **MR 2** | 2 |
| **MR 3** | 2 |
| **MR 4** | 3 |
| **MR 5** | 3 |
| **MR 6** | 4 |

A character may slot only this many Passives as their prepared Passive loadout.

All slotted Passives are active.

There is no separate “prepared but inactive” Passive state.

If a Passive is not slotted, it provides no benefit.

Passive choices cannot be changed during combat.

## Allowed Passive Categories

Every Passive must belong to exactly one Passive Category.

A Passive Category is a real mechanical classification, not flavor text.  
Each Passive should express one clear character identity and should not hide an Active effect inside an always-on feature.

The current allowed Passive Categories are:

| **Category** | **Role** | **Typical Effects** |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Armor** | Flat physical mitigation and toughness. | +Armor, Armor stance, conditional Armor |
| **Damage Reduction** | Percentage-based post-Armor mitigation. | 10% / 20% / 30% DR |
| **Evade** | Avoidance and defensive positioning. | +Evade, conditional Evade |
| **Damage** | Pure offensive output. | +Damage dice, conditional Damage |
| **Health** | Structural durability. | Additional Health Bars |
| **Temporary HP** | Frontloaded defensive buffer. | Start-of-combat Temporary HP |
| **Healing** | Recovery and regeneration. | Start-of-turn healing, conditional healing |
| **Awareness** | Combat senses and perception replacement. | Combat Sense, sensory replacement under obstruction |
| **Phasing** | Premium hit-negation defense. | Ignore limited hits per combat |
| **Special Aura** | The only approved Passive interaction with Specials. | Increases one chosen existing eligible Special(X) by +1 inside a self-centered aura |

## Passive Category: Special Aura

**Special Aura** is finalized and is the only Passive category allowed to interact with Specials.

A Special Aura never applies a Special by itself. It only increases one chosen existing eligible **Special(X)** by **+1 step** while an already affected creature remains inside the aura.

Use the complete Special Aura rules and progression later in this catalogue.

## Passive Limits

Passives may not grant:

- permanent Attack Dice,
- permanent Attribute increases,
- Attribute Dice,
- Stones,
- generic Roll bonuses,
- generic Control effects,
- free attacks,
- Extra Attacks,
- or passive action-economy effects.

A Passive may not apply Specials. Any approved Passive interaction with Specials must use the **Special Aura** category and may only increase one existing eligible Special(X) by +1 inside its aura.

A Passive may not grant Stunned or any other hard-control Special.

## Passive Design Restrictions

- A Passive may never use the **Spell** tag.
- A Passive may never use the **Charged** tag.
- A Passive may never grant an Attack Action, free attack, Reaction, Movement Power, Spell effect, Barrier, Wall, Image, Illusion Field, Summon, or Persistent Zone.
- A Passive may not hide an Active effect inside an always-on feature.
- A Passive may not grant **Stunned** or any other hard-control Special.
- A Passive may not create full-turn skip, full-round invulnerability, or automatic hard control.
- A Passive may not casually combine Armor, Evade, Temporary HP, Healing, Damage Reduction, and Phasing into one package.
- Unused PP may remain unused for premium milestone systems.

## Passive Pricing Modes

Some Passive categories may be built in either an **unconditional** or **conditional** form.

An **unconditional Passive** applies broadly, reliably, or without meaningful tactical effort.  
Because it is always available, it costs **twice** the normal baseline value.

A **conditional Passive** applies only while a clear, meaningful combat condition is met.  
Because the player must earn, create, or maintain that condition, it uses the normal baseline value.

| **Passive Mode** | **Cost Modifier** | **Design Meaning** |
|:--|:--:|:--|
| **Unconditional** | ×2 baseline cost | Reliable, broad, always-on value |
| **Conditional** | ×1 baseline cost | Stronger value, but requires real tactical play |

The following categories use this pricing model:

| **Category** | **Unconditional Cost** | **Conditional Cost** |
|:--|:--:|:--:|
| **Damage** | 40 PP per +1d6 Damage | 20 PP per +1d6 Damage |
| **Armor** | 15 PP per +1 Armor | 7.5 PP per +1 Armor |
| **Evade** | 10 PP per +1 Evade | 5 PP per +1 Evade |
| **Healing** | 8 PP per 1 HP healed | 4 PP per 1 HP healed |

Unconditional Passives belong in the main Passive Catalog.  
Conditional Passives are listed in their own section after the main catalog.

## Closed Defensive Subsystems

### Damage Reduction

Damage Reduction is a closed defensive subsystem.

Only the following Power lines may grant, increase, or otherwise enable Damage Reduction:

1. **Damage Reduction (Passive)**
2. **Unyielding Shell (Active Buff)**
3. **Unyielding Intercept (Reaction)**

No other Passive, Active, Active Buff, Reaction, Movement Power, Spell, Special, rider, aura, item-like effect, or combination entry may grant Damage Reduction, increase Damage Reduction, or simulate Damage Reduction under a different name.

Damage Reduction may not appear as a secondary rider.  
Damage Reduction may not be bundled with Armor, Evade, Temporary HP, Healing, Phasing, or any other filler value.

### Phasing

Phasing is a closed defensive subsystem.

Only the following Power lines may grant, increase, or otherwise enable defensive Phasing:

1. **Ghostform (Passive)**
2. **Ghost Mantle (Active Buff)**
3. **Ghost Slip (Reaction, One-Time-Use)**

No other Passive, Active, Active Buff, Reaction, Movement Power, Spell, Special, rider, aura, item-like effect, or combination entry may grant Phasing, increase Phasing, or simulate Phasing under a different name.

Phasing may not appear as a secondary rider.  
Phasing may not be bundled with Armor, Damage Reduction, Evade, Temporary HP, Healing, or any other filler value.

### Combat Sense

A **Combat Sense** is a sense that may replace sight for combat purposes.

If a creature can reasonably be perceived by one of your Combat Senses, you may target it, attack it, defend against it, and use Reactions against it without needing sight.

A Combat Sense does not automatically reveal hidden creatures. If a creature is actively hiding, you must still detect it with an appropriate check.

A Combat Sense does not ignore Total Cover, does not bypass Phasing, and does not automatically disbelieve illusions. It only changes which sense you may use to perceive and fight.

#### Available Combat Senses

| **Combat Sense** | **What It Perceives** | **Common Limits** |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Hearing** | Sound, movement, voice, breathing, impact, armor noise, spellcasting words, weapon motion, and other audible cues. | Fails against silence, soundless targets, overwhelming noise, or barriers that block sound. |
| **Touch / Vibration** | Contact, pressure, air movement, ground vibration, surface vibration, footfalls, nearby movement, and physical disturbance. | Fails against targets with no shared medium, fully airborne targets, phasing targets, or effects that isolate vibration. |
| **Scent** | Smell, blood, sweat, breath, smoke, decay, poison traces, tracks, and other scent trails. | Fails against scentless targets, overwhelming smells, sealed environments, strong wind, or effects that mask scent. |
| **Presence Sense** | Living, undead, spiritual, marked, soul-bearing, or supernatural presence. | Fails against truly empty targets, specially concealed presences, some constructs, Total Cover where presence cannot reach, or effects that hide the soul/signature. Presence Sense may only be chosen at Level 12 or higher. |

## Combined Passives

Combined Passives blend exactly two existing Passive axes into one Passive.

They are meant for hybrid character concepts: not as strong in one axis as a pure Passive, but broader and more flexible.

A Combined Passive still occupies one Passive slot.

For loadout and stacking purposes, a Combined Passive counts as **both included Passive categories**.

Example:

> **Passive: Armor / Temporary HP** counts as both **Armor** and **Temporary HP**.  
> You cannot also benefit from another active **Armor** Passive or another active **Temporary HP** Passive at the same time.

## Combined Passive Rules

A Combined Passive:

- combines exactly **two** Passive axes,
- uses the Passive Curve,
- splits its value between both axes,
- may not add a third mechanical axis,
- may not hide an Active effect,
- may not grant free attacks, Actions, Reactions, Movement Powers, Summons, Images, Barriers, Walls, or Persistent Zones.

Combined Passives may use:

- **Armor**
- **Evade**
- **Damage**
- **Health**
- **Temporary HP**
- **Healing**
- **Awareness**

Combined Passives may not use:

- **Damage Reduction**
- **Phasing**
- **Special**, until Special Passive rules are finalized
- **Stunned**
- **Attack Dice**
- **Attribute bonuses**
- **generic Roll bonuses**

## Conditional Combined Passives

Conditional Combined Passives blend exactly two existing Passive axes into one Passive, but only while a meaningful combat condition is met.

They are stronger than unconditional Combined Passives because the character must earn, create, or maintain the condition.

A Conditional Combined Passive still occupies one Passive slot.

For loadout and stacking purposes, a Conditional Combined Passive counts as **both included Passive categories**.

Example:

> **Conditional Passive: Armor / Healing** counts as both **Armor** and **Healing**.  
> You cannot also benefit from another active **Armor** Passive or another active **Healing** Passive at the same time.

## Conditional Combined Passive Rules

A Conditional Combined Passive:

- combines exactly **two** Passive axes,
- uses the Passive Curve,
- uses the conditional baseline for each included axis,
- splits its value between both axes,
- requires a clear combat condition,
- may not add a third mechanical axis,
- may not hide an Active effect,
- may not grant free attacks, Actions, Reactions, Movement Powers, Summons, Images, Barriers, Walls, or Persistent Zones.

Conditional Combined Passives may use:

- **Armor**
- **Evade**
- **Damage**
- **Health**
- **Temporary HP**
- **Healing**
- **Awareness**

Conditional Combined Passives may not use:

- **Damage Reduction**
- **Phasing**
- **Special**, until Special Passive rules are finalized
- **Stunned**
- **Attack Dice**
- **Attribute bonuses**
- **generic Roll bonuses**

| **Awareness Half** | Milestone Combat Sense progression; not priced as flat dice |

### Conditional Health / Temporary HP Timing

If a Conditional Combined Passive grants **Temporary HP**, check its condition at the start of combat or at the first listed trigger.  
If the condition is true, gain the Temporary HP once. It does not refresh.

If a Conditional Combined Passive grants **Health Bars**, check its condition at the start of combat.  
If the condition is true, gain the listed Health Bars for that combat. These Health Bars are structural for that combat and do not appear or disappear round by round.

## Passive Special Aura

**Special Aura** is the only allowed Passive form that may interact with Specials.

A Passive Special Aura is a self-centered aura that increases one chosen eligible **Special(X)** by **+1 step** while an already affected creature remains inside the aura.

It never applies the Special by itself.

It never refreshes, extends, spreads, triggers, or maintains the Special.

If the creature leaves the aura, the increase immediately ends.

Multiple Special Auras affecting the same Special do not stack. Only the strongest applicable aura applies.

#### Example — Weaken Aura

A creature inside the aura is affected by **Weaken(4)**. The Passive Special Aura increases it to **Weaken(5)** while that creature remains inside the aura. When it leaves, the Special returns to its normal current value. The aura never applies Weaken to an unaffected creature.

#### Example — Soulburn Aura

A creature inside the aura is affected by **Soulburn(6)**. The aura treats it as **Soulburn(7)** while the creature remains inside. The +1 increase ends immediately when the creature leaves the aura.

## Special Aura Category Rule

A Passive Special Aura uses the following category:

> **Category:** Special Aura

or in table format:

> **Type:** Passive  
> **Category:** Special Aura

Special Aura is a real Passive category.

It is not a generic Special category and does not allow other Passive Special designs.

## Eligible Specials for Passive Special Aura

A Passive Special Aura may only affect a Special if all of the following are true:

- the Special is written as **Special(X)**,
- the Special uses diminishing scaling,
- increasing X by +1 is meaningful,
- the Special is not binary,
- the Special is not hard control,
- the Special does not remove a full turn,
- the Special does not deny all actions,
- the Special does not deny all reactions,
- the Special does not grant or alter Damage Reduction or Phasing.

Common eligible examples include:

- **Blight(X)**
- **Challenge(X)**
- **Corrode(X)**
- **Disoriented(X)**
- **Expose(X)**
- **Hex(X)**
- **Lacerate(X)**
- **Mark(X)**
- **Ruin(X)**
- **Slow(X)**
- **Soulburn(X)**
- **Sundered(X)**
- **Weaken(X)**

A Passive Special Aura may not:

- apply a Special,
- add a Special to an attack,
- add a Special to all damage,
- increase more than one Special,

- affect hard control,
- affect full-turn denial,
- affect Damage Reduction,
- affect Phasing,
- affect Barriers, Walls, Images, Summons, Illusion Fields, or Persistent Zones.

A Passive Special Aura always grants exactly **+1 step** to one chosen eligible **Special(X)**.

It never scales the Special bonus beyond +1.

Scaling is handled through aura radius only.

Passive Special Aura Core = **80 PP**.

Final cost:

> **Final Cost = 80 PP + Radius Cost**

Use the normal radius-cost table for the aura.

The aura is always centered on the user.

## Parry Passives

Parry is a dedicated defensive subsystem and one of the primary Defense Pillars of the Mastery System.

Unlike **Evade**, which sets the Target Number an Attack must overcome, Parry directly disrupts the incoming Attack before it is rolled.

Parry removes **Attack Dice** from an eligible Attack Pool.

A partially parried Attack may still hit or miss normally. An Attack whose entire Attack Pool is removed is **Fully Parried** and does not resolve.

Parry does not require a Contest, defensive roll, or comparison against the Attack result. It is resolved entirely through the character's Parry Pool.

Only characters with an appropriate **Parry Passive** may use this subsystem.

---

### Parry Requirements

Parry requires a suitable melee weapon, shield, natural weapon, or another implement explicitly capable of parrying the triggering Attack.

When learning a Parry Passive, choose either **Might** or **Agility** as your **Parry Attribute**.

The chosen Attribute cannot normally be changed unless the Power is retrained.

A weapon, shield, natural weapon, Artifact, Passive, or other rule may define which kinds of direct Attacks it is capable of parrying.

---

### Entering Parry

At the beginning of your Turn, you may enter **Parry** until the beginning of your next Turn.

Entering Parry represents committing your Turn to active defense.

While Parrying:

* You cannot use an **Attack Action**, including additional Attack Actions.
* You may use your Movement normally.
* You may use Reactions normally.
* You gain a **Parry Pool** equal to your chosen Parry Attribute, up to the maximum allowed by your Parry Passive's Level.

A character cannot enter Parry after using an Attack Action during the same Turn.

---

### Spending Parry

When an eligible direct Attack targets you, the attacker first declares the Attack, its targets, any Raises, and any division of the Attack Pool.

Before the Attack Roll is made, you may spend points from your Parry Pool.

For every point of Parry spent:

* Remove **1 Attack Die** from the Attack Pool assigned to you.
* Removed Attack Dice are not rolled.

You choose how many points to spend against each eligible Attack.

Your Parry Pool is shared across all eligible Attacks against you until the beginning of your next Turn.

If Parry reduces an Attack Pool below its normal Keep value, roll the remaining Attack Dice and keep all remaining dice.

#### Example

An enemy attacks with **8k3**.

You spend 6 Parry and remove 6 Attack Dice.

The Attack becomes **2k2**.

The enemy rolls the remaining 2 dice and keeps both.

---

### Fully Parried Attacks

If you reduce the triggering Attack Pool to **0 Attack Dice**, the Attack is **Fully Parried**.

A Fully Parried Attack:

* makes no Attack Roll,
* does not hit,
* rolls no Damage Dice,
* deals no damage,
* applies no Specials,
* applies no on-hit effects,
* and does not resolve any effect that requires the Attack to hit.

A Fully Parried Attack may trigger abilities such as **Riposte** or **Reflection**.

Unspent Parry is lost at the beginning of your next Turn.

### Parry Limitations

Parry normally applies only to eligible direct Attacks that make an Attack Roll against you.

Parry does not normally affect:

* Area Attacks
* Area Effects
* environmental damage
* ongoing damage
* automatic damage
* Persistent Zones
* effects that do not make an Attack Roll
* Attacks that the wielded implement is not capable of parrying

A specific Passive, Active Buff, Reaction, Artifact, weapon, shield, natural weapon, or other ability may explicitly expand which Attacks can be parried.

Parry does not increase Evade and does not reduce Damage Dice.

It modifies the incoming Attack Pool before the Attack Roll is made.

## Absorption Passives

Absorption is a closed premium Passive subsystem built around accepting real physical injury and converting that suffering into immediate power.

Unlike other defensive systems, Absorption does not prevent damage.

* **Evade** avoids the Attack.
* **Parry** removes incoming Damage Dice.
* **Armor** reduces rolled damage.
* **Damage Reduction** reduces remaining damage.
* **Phasing** ignores an incoming hit.
* **Absorption** allows the damage to enter the character's Health Bars.

An Absorption character is designed to withstand significantly more physical punishment than a normal character. As the character loses real HP, that damage generates short-lived **Temporary Colorless Stones** that may be used to fuel existing Stone Abilities.

Absorption does not create a separate resource pool, charge system, or list of unique expenditures. It uses the existing Stone economy.

Only the dedicated **Absorption Passive** may generate Temporary Colorless Stones from lost HP.

---

### Absorption Health

Absorption increases the amount of real physical damage the character can sustain.

While the Absorption Passive is slotted, each normal Health Bar gains additional Maximum HP according to the Passive's Level.

The size of each normal Health Bar becomes:

`Vitality × 2 + Absorption HP`

The final **Incapacitated** box remains unchanged.

The additional HP granted by Absorption are real HP.

They:

* are part of the character's normal Health Bars,
* may be restored through normal Healing,
* count as actual HP loss when damaged,
* may generate Temporary Colorless Stones,
* do not function as Temporary HP,
* do not create a separate Health Pool,
* do not prevent Health Penalties,
* and do not restore destroyed or Scarred Health Bars.

---

}}
\page
{{pageNumber,auto}}
{{wide
### Eligible Absorption Damage

Absorption counts only damage that actually removes current HP from one of the character's Health Bars.

Resolve the damage normally.

Only the final amount entered as HP loss counts as **Absorbed Damage**.

The following does not generate Absorbed Damage:

* Damage prevented by Evade
* Damage Dice removed through Parry
* Damage prevented by Armor
* Damage prevented by Damage Reduction
* Attacks ignored through Phasing
* Attacks returned through Reflection
* Damage applied to Temporary HP
* Damage prevented by resistance or immunity
* Self-inflicted damage
* HP paid as a cost
* Damage caused by the character's own Powers
* Damage intentionally caused by a willing ally

Only hostile damage caused by an enemy or hostile combat effect may generate Absorbed Damage.

---

### Absorption Threshold

Track eligible HP lost while the Absorption Passive is slotted.

Whenever the accumulated Absorbed Damage reaches the character's **Vitality**, reduce the accumulated amount by the character's Vitality and gain **1 Temporary Colorless Stone**.

Excess Absorbed Damage carries over toward the next Stone.

There is no limit to how many Temporary Colorless Stones may be generated from a single damage instance, provided enough actual HP are lost.

#### Example

A character has **Vitality 8**.

The character loses **30 actual HP** from a hostile Attack.

* At 8 damage, the character gains the first Temporary Colorless Stone.
* At 16 damage, the character gains the second Stone.
* At 24 damage, the character gains the third Stone.
* The remaining 6 damage carries over toward the next Stone.

The character therefore gains **3 Temporary Colorless Stones** and retains **6 accumulated Absorbed Damage**.

### Temporary Colorless Stones

Temporary Colorless Stones represent unstable power created through immediate physical suffering.

A Temporary Colorless Stone may be spent as a Stone of any Attribute when paying for an eligible Stone Ability.

Temporary Colorless Stones follow these rules:

* They are gained in the **Ready** state.
* They may be spent during the Round in which they are gained.
* They remain available until the end of the character's next Turn.
* Any unused Temporary Colorless Stones disappear at the end of that Turn.
* When spent, they disappear instead of becoming Exhausted.
* They do not regenerate.
* They cannot be Sealed, Bound, Burned, transferred, stored, or invested.
* They do not satisfy permanent Stone requirements.
* They cannot be used outside combat.
* They cannot remain available beyond the end of the character's next Turn.

Accumulated Absorbed Damage that has not yet reached the character's Vitality remains until another eligible damage instance is suffered.

All remaining accumulated Absorbed Damage disappears when combat ends.

---

### Closed Premium Subsystem

Absorption is a closed premium subsystem.

Only the dedicated **Absorption Passive** may:

* increase Health Bar size through Absorption HP,
* track Absorbed Damage,
* or generate Temporary Colorless Stones from lost HP.

No other Passive, Active, Active Buff, Reaction, Movement Power, Spell, Artifact Function, Special, rider, or item-like effect may generate Temporary Colorless Stones from damage unless it explicitly belongs to the Absorption subsystem.

Absorption may not appear as a secondary rider on another Passive.

## Damage Negation Passives

Damage Negation is a closed premium defensive subsystem that reduces incoming damage before it is rolled.

Unlike **Parry**, Damage Negation does not interfere with the Attack Pool and cannot prevent an Attack from hitting.

Unlike **Armor**, Damage Negation does not reduce the rolled damage result.

Unlike **Damage Reduction**, Damage Negation does not reduce damage by a percentage after the roll.

Instead, Damage Negation removes a limited number of **Damage Dice** from incoming Damage Pools before those dice are rolled.

Damage Negation uses a limited reserve that is shared across all eligible damage instances during a Combat.

Only characters with the dedicated **Damage Negation Passive** have a Damage Negation Reserve.

---

### Damage Negation Reserve

While the Damage Negation Passive is slotted, you gain a **Damage Negation Reserve** according to the Passive's Level at the beginning of each Combat.

The Reserve represents the total number of Damage Dice you may remove during that Combat.

The Reserve:

* begins at its maximum at the beginning of each Combat,
* is shared across all eligible damage instances during that Combat,
* does not refresh at the beginning of a Round,
* does not refresh when a new enemy enters the existing Combat,
* does not refresh during a new phase or wave of the same encounter,
* and is lost when the Combat ends.

A new Reserve is gained only when a genuinely new Combat begins.

Spending Damage Negation does not require an Action, Movement, Reaction, Stone, or roll.

You choose when and how much of the Reserve to spend.

---

### Spending Damage Negation

When an eligible Damage Pool would be rolled against you, you may spend points from your Damage Negation Reserve before the damage is rolled.

For every point spent:

* Remove **1 Damage Die** from the Damage Pool assigned to you.
* Removed Damage Dice are not rolled.
* Reduce your remaining Damage Negation Reserve by 1.

Damage Negation affects only the Damage Pool assigned to you.

It does not reduce damage dealt to other creatures by the same Attack, Area Effect, or shared Damage Pool.

If the same Damage Pool would normally be rolled once for several creatures, roll a separate reduced Damage Pool for the character using Damage Negation.

---

### Half-Pool Limit

Damage Negation can never remove more than half of the original Damage Dice assigned to you.

The maximum number of Damage Dice that may be removed is:

`Original Damage Dice ÷ 2, rounded down`

At least half of the original Damage Dice, rounded up, must remain.

This limit applies to the total amount removed by all Damage Negation effects combined.

}}

{{wide

#### Examples

An Attack would deal **12d8 damage**.

* You may remove no more than **6 Damage Dice**.
* At least **6d8 damage** must still be rolled.

An Attack would deal **7d8 damage**.

* You may remove no more than **3 Damage Dice**.
* At least **4d8 damage** must still be rolled.

An effect would deal **1d8 damage**.

* No Damage Dice may be removed.
* The original **1d8** must still be rolled.

---

### Damage Negation Timing

Damage Negation is resolved after an Attack has hit or another effect has successfully created a Damage Pool, but before that Damage Pool is rolled.

The normal defensive order is:

1. Build the Attack Pool.
2. Apply Parry and other Attack Pool modifications.
3. Roll the Attack against Evade.
4. Determine whether the Attack hits.
5. Resolve Phasing or another effect that completely ignores the hit.
6. Build the Damage Pool assigned to the character.
7. Apply Damage Negation.
8. Roll the remaining Damage Dice.
9. Apply Armor.
10. Apply Damage Reduction.
11. Apply Temporary HP and other legal damage buffers.
12. Apply the remaining damage to current HP.
13. Determine whether actual HP loss triggers Absorption.

If an Attack is Fully Parried, misses, or is ignored through Phasing, no Damage Pool is rolled and no Damage Negation needs to be spent.

---

### Attack Effects and Specials

Damage Negation affects only Damage Dice.

It does not alter whether the triggering Attack hit.

Damage Negation does not prevent or remove:

* Specials
* on-hit effects
* forced movement
* control effects
* Marks
* conditions
* ongoing effects
* flat damage
* automatic effects
* or another payload that does not depend on the number of Damage Dice rolled

}}

{{wide

A Special or on-hit effect applies normally unless another defense, such as **Ward**, prevents or reduces it.

Reducing an Attack's Damage Pool does not cause the Attack to miss.

---

### Eligible Damage

Damage Negation may be used against any damage instance that rolls Damage Dice and assigns those Damage Dice to the character.

This may include:

* direct Attacks
* Area Attacks
* Area Effects
* hostile environmental damage
* ongoing damage that rolls Damage Dice
* other hostile effects that create a Damage Pool

Damage Negation cannot affect:

* flat damage without Damage Dice
* HP loss that is not damage
* HP paid as a cost
* self-inflicted damage
* Healing
* Temporary HP loss that is not caused by a Damage Pool
* effects that do not roll Damage Dice

---

### Interaction with Other Defenses

Damage Negation may be combined with the primary Defense Pillars and other premium defenses.

#### Parry

Parry removes **Attack Dice** before the Attack Roll.

Damage Negation removes **Damage Dice** after the Attack has hit but before damage is rolled.

A partially parried Attack that still hits may be reduced through Damage Negation.

Damage Negation cannot cause a Fully Parried result and cannot trigger Riposte or Reflection.

#### Armor

Damage Negation removes Damage Dice before the damage roll.

Armor reduces the rolled damage result afterward.

#### Damage Reduction

Damage Negation removes Damage Dice before the damage roll.

Damage Reduction reduces the remaining damage after Armor.

#### Phasing

Phasing ignores the entire hit.

If Phasing is used, no Damage Negation needs to be spent against that hit.

#### Absorption

Damage Negation reduces the amount of damage that may eventually remove actual HP.

Damage prevented through Damage Negation does not count as actual HP loss and cannot generate Temporary Colorless Stones through Absorption.

A Damage Negation Reserve exists only for the duration of the current Combat.

The Reserve does not refresh merely because:

* a new Round begins,
* Initiative is recalculated,
* reinforcements arrive,
* a boss changes phase,
* enemies retreat temporarily while the encounter continues,
* or the battlefield changes.

These events remain part of the same Combat unless the Game Master determines that the encounter has genuinely ended.

When the Combat ends:

* all unspent Damage Negation is lost,
* the Reserve is removed,
* and no remaining points may be carried into another Combat.

At the beginning of the next Combat, the character gains a new Reserve at its full listed value.

* increase a Damage Negation Reserve,
* generate temporary Damage Negation,
* restore spent Damage Negation,

* Evade

* or another filler effect

unless a dedicated Damage Negation entry explicitly states otherwise.
